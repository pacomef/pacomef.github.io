Quick summary:
- I took part in the 2026 European finals of competitive programming (ICPC EUC), which gathers the top 150 students in Europe.
- I regularly take part in online contests, whether they run for a few hours or several days.
- I qualified for the Prologin finals twice (out of two attempts).
</div>


<span class="underline">High school:</span> I got into competitive programming with the Google Kickstart contests, around 2019 I think, and quickly realized I loved it, since I'd wake up at 3 or 4 in the morning to take part in contests running on US time. It was after one of these contests that I discovered Project Euler, in 2020, which is probably a bit less competitive, but definitely in the same spirit.

<span class="underline">Classes préparatoires:</span> At the start of my prépa I discovered Prologin (thanks to classmates who were better informed than me), a French algorithmic contest run in 3 phases, the first two of which are pure competitive programming. I took part for 2 years and qualified for the final both times, even though the final itself moves a bit away from competitive programming.

<span class="underline">Engineering school:</span> At the end of prépa, I discovered the international contests open to higher-education students, like SWERC, the first selection stage in Western Europe to access other competitions. With that in mind, I chose between the two main options available to me: Télécom Paris and Centrale Supélec — I picked Télécom Paris because Centrale doesn't send teams to these contests (or only rarely).

I took part in SWERC 2024, where my team put in a decent performance, but nothing amazing.

I took part in SWERC 2025 again, with a different team, and with a bit more training before the competition, we managed to finish 14th! That got us (just barely) qualified for the EUC, the European selection stage before the world finals. It was held in Warsaw, in February 2026, and I'm honestly honored to have been able to take part. Only 50 teams from all of Europe compete there, and the only French teams were ENS Ulm, École Polytechnique, Télécom Paris, and INSA Lyon.

![EUC 2026 contest room in Warsaw]({{ "/_images/icpc/room.JPEG" | relative_url }})

On site, a lot of activities were organized, but unfortunately we didn't manage to qualify for the world finals. Either way, that wasn't really our goal — we knew it was better to simply enjoy the moment, so we went all in on solving problem I, which was only solved by two teams, which didn't exactly give us a great ranking! Still, I'll remember these 4 days as a wonderful experience, and I hope to be able to do it again from my current university: Aalto (in Helsinki, Finland).
